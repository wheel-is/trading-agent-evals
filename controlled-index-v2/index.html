03 / Methodology audit

What went wrong

Capture evidence was overinterpreted

The old validator checked metadata status, timestamps and hashes, but did not require a task-specific terminal signal. Jango’s “initialization complete” and “idle—waiting for the next wake” are not sufficient proof of a final response or timeout. The third batch was stopped after roughly 4–5 minutes, before its 20-minute window expired; the repeat had the same problem.

The first batch also had an operator pause/resume. The second answer was recovered after a long observer gap, and its availability was inferred from an initialization timestamp. Both answers remain useful content evidence, with unresolved execution comparability.

The task set hit a ceiling

The 66 cases are six variants of eleven templates, given in three large primary packs. They provide explicit data, formulas, thresholds and output schemas. They do not test discovery of missing evidence, messy live sources, sustained monitoring, or performance under changing market conditions.

All checks passing really does produce 100 under the frozen rule. The right response is a harder, separately frozen holdout with broader capabilities—not subtracting points after seeing who passed.

Jango evidence decisions

core-1The operator mistook the start of an answer for completion, paused, then resumed. The complete 22-case response was recovered within the budget and matches every key, but this was an altered execution, not a clean trial.
core-2The complete 22-case answer was recovered after an approximately eight-hour observer gap. The receipt used the Initialization complete timestamp to infer availability within 20 minutes. That event does not prove the final answer was available then. Its content remains correct; its measured timing eligibility is unresolved.
core-3Only an introductory promise was captured. Initialization complete and idle—waiting for the next wake do not prove this asynchronous task was terminal. The operator paused it before the 1,200-second observation window expired. An uncertain observer outcome was incorrectly treated as a measured target failure.
repeatOnly an introductory promise was captured. Initialization complete and idle—waiting for the next wake do not prove this asynchronous task was terminal. The operator paused it before the 1,200-second observation window expired. An uncertain observer outcome was incorrectly treated as a measured target failure.

A strict format rule overwhelmed content

A malformed JSON envelope zeroed a whole 22-case pack. Muse and oh-my-pi therefore look much weaker on the old composite than on recoverable calculations. This rule was applied consistently, but it is a poor stand-in for trading-research quality. Delivery, schema compliance and substantive accuracy need distinct reporting.

What this audit does not prove: that Jango is best, that the other providers’ correct answers are wrong, that every oracle has been independently validated, or that all local/account contexts were fully isolated. Three primary sessions cannot support a conclusive population ranking.

04 / Requirements before another quality ranking

Make the next result harder to fool

  1. Validate every capture adapter. Pilot final-answer, refusal, timeout, delayed-delivery and idle-state cases. Require a final response, a task-specific terminal failure, or observation through the full deadline. Operator interventions and observer gaps remain unresolved.
  2. Calibrate difficulty on a development set. Use easy, medium and hard cases covering temporal source conflicts, entity resolution, missing data, constrained decisions, uncertainty and multi-step state changes. Freeze an independently generated holdout before any scored responses. Do not change it to obtain a preferred winner.
  3. Use independent tasks and planned repeats. One task per fresh session, randomized order and at least three prespecified trials per task/configuration. Treat task families as clusters; do not count fields or repeated captures as independent samples.
  4. Separate the measurements. Publish substantive accuracy, evidence support, uncertainty handling, delivery reliability and format compliance. A declared 0–100 primary index needs fixed weights and handling of failures before collection; conditional diagnostics cannot replace it.
  5. Verify the references and the claims. Independently check deterministic keys, archive dated primary sources, calibrate blinded semantic judging against adjudicated examples, and use a simulation with observable state for autonomy tests. Financial truth cannot be established from agreement among models.
  6. Release only supported comparisons. Preserve all attempts, exact configurations and source hashes; report uncertainty and missing providers. A ceiling or overlapping uncertainty means “not separated,” even if a numeric score exists.

These are design requirements for a new cohort, not a claim that those experiments have already been run. A perfect holdout score remains possible; difficulty must never be tuned after the results.
