METHOD AUDIT · WHAT IS IMPLEMENTED AND WHAT IS STILL MISSING

Rigor, difficulty and defensibility

This run is an inspectable exploratory comparison. It does not yet justify a validated financial leaderboard or a state-of-the-art evaluation claim.

What changed in report 1.4

Jango is now visible in the main result chart, all-test matrix and skill breakdowns, with a separate 245-check paper assessment. Correctness with critical gates is the default. The 121 research cases, original 157-check rubric, candidate labels and weights are unchanged. Eight skill categories and five task families are presentation-only, added after collection. No product was rerun and no old answer was judged against new harder requirements.

What the scores mean

For each axis, passed, failed and unknown weights stay in the denominator: lower = 100P/(P+F+U), upper = 100(P+U)/(P+F+U). A failed critical check zeros the task’s gated correctness. An unknown critical check keeps its lower bound at zero. Unknown is neither a pass nor a proven product error. Full-task all-pass requires every required content criterion. Our intervals represent unresolved labels, not sampling uncertainty.

Each task has equal weight in the common P02–P11 mean. P01 pilots stay separate. A skill sub-score first uses original weights within a task, then averages participating tasks equally. Empty skills are N/A. No relevance/correctness composite is introduced; paper operations never enter the financial score. Tables retain counts so a small category cannot look like a broad benchmark.

Why 100% appeared—and what difficulty requires

The prior default measured task relevance. A response can fully acknowledge the mandate, explain a method and honestly report unresolved data while earning 100% relevance. That is a ceiling in a secondary compliance measure, not perfect financial performance. No research case has an established full financial all-pass. A concrete harder next-run protocol

Before another product run, each task needs three development fixture families: a complete solvable case, a forensic boundary case, and a paired robustness case. Complete source-provided reasoning tasks and open-web retrieval tasks should be separate. Pilot difficulty on development examples with strong systems and qualified analysts, then freeze private date/entity variants, sources, criteria, budgets and selection rules. Three planned isolated trials per fixture/configuration are a starting design; observed variance determines whether more are needed. This is a protocol, not an executed new benchmark.

The original release gate remains blocked. There are zero qualified-expert calibration labels and zero fully validated historical task packs. Judge and sampling limitations

The original review has 1620 unanimous decisions out of 1,727, including unanimous unknowns. Agreement is not accuracy. All three review passes used the same inherited model; exact model identity and distinct model families were not verified. The Jango passes likewise use three fresh independent contexts within that model, and are unblinded to product identity. Product names were masked for the research pass but blinding effectiveness was not tested. New paper labels use the same conservative disagreement rule: pass/fail splits and critical non-unanimity remain unknown.

Most targets have one selected trial per test. Three judges are not three target trials. Neither repeated screenshots nor retries increase the independent sample size. Mixed native tools, context, cutoffs and resource limits prevent model-only causal comparisons. We report no standard error, statistical winner, population-level superiority, or investment-performance result.

Connection to published evaluation practice

Vals Finance Agent v2 uses weighted critical checks, partial credit and all-pass, analytical categories, repeated target runs and a model jury. Its documented method also uses expert-reviewed held-out data and a shared harness. We borrow the reporting and scoring ideas, but this mixed-product collection has not reproduced those validation conditions. The original local 1/3/5 weights and calibration thresholds are design choices.

Anthropic’s agent-evaluation guidance supports choosing deterministic, model and human graders for the type of evidence and reviewing actual trajectories. FActScore motivates atomic source-grounded claims. HELM motivates reporting multiple dimensions and coverage. These references inform the design; citing them does not certify this run.

Reproducibility and evidence

Research labels and original inputs are hash-preserved. New Jango criteria were frozen before its new votes; the adaptation is explicitly post-hoc. New text quotes are checked against retained source bytes and stored with SHA-256 and UTF-8 spans. Image-only quotes are checked against actual screenshot pixels, with separate visual-validation records and no invented text-byte spans. Public excerpts omit account identifiers and local user paths; the complete original evidence remains in the local export. The score calculator is the unchanged original engine. Presentation tests check track separation, P01 denominators, unknowns and category mappings.
